## How to read this inventory

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ID** | Canonical source ID (`CS-nn`). Cite this ID in every other Chris deliverable so each claim traces back to one verified source. |
| **Evidence strength** | Judged for the specific claims this source is used to support. A split rating (e.g. "High (normative) / Low (empirical)") means the source is authoritative about what *should* happen but does not measure what *does* happen. |
| **Included** | Admissible as core evidence. |
| **Included (context only)** | May be cited for background, provenance or framing. **Must not carry a load-bearing evidential claim.** |
| **Excluded** | Not admissible as core evidence. |

### Collection and verification method

Sources were gathered by seven parallel evidence-gathering workstreams (stakeholders and governance; stakeholder journey and contact points; communication channels and fallback; service logistics and coordination risk; adoption, trust and behavioural response; CSAT-style evaluation instruments; Thailand and Southeast Asia context), then consolidated and adversarially re-verified by two quality-review passes. Search terms, platforms and inclusion decisions are recorded in `Chris_research_log.md`.

Every source was opened before being recorded. Where full text could not be retrieved, that is stated in the source's own summary file and the claim is restricted to what was actually seen. **No citation, DOI, author, year or statistic in this inventory was generated from memory.**

## Known bias in this evidence base

The same publisher domains blocked automated retrieval across every workstream: PubMed and PMC (reCAPTCHA), ScienceDirect and Europe PMC (robots.txt), WHO IRIS, Taylor & Francis, Wiley, MIS Quarterly, ACM Digital Library, AMS Journals and several AHRQ PDF paths (HTTP 403). **This inventory is therefore skewed toward open-access and government-hosted material.** Named, directly relevant sources that were consequently never opened and are cited nowhere in the Chris deliverables include: Assan, Khosravi & Osei (2026) in *Energy Research & Social Science*; Sutton et al. on 360- vs 90-character WEA messages; LeClerc & Joslyn (2015) "The Cry Wolf Effect and Weather-Related Decision Making"; the 2021 Pacific Northwest heat-dome Washington healthcare qualitative study (PMID 40246560); "Heat-related health impacts in ASEAN countries: A scoping review"; Zander et al. on German public preferences for heat-warning timing; Bangor, Kortum & Miller (2008); and Joint Commission Sentinel Event Alert 58.

**Highest-value outstanding retrieval:** the full 272-page WHO Europe *Heat–health action plans: guidance, second edition* (CS-03). Three workstreams independently hit HTTP 403 on every route. Its sector action briefs, public health message bank, stakeholder matrices and evaluation framework are all unverified, and it is the single largest substantive gap in this evidence base. A team member with institutional or library access should retrieve it before Pitch 2.
