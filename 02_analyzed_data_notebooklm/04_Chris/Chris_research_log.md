## Method

Evidence was gathered by seven parallel workstreams, then consolidated and independently verified by two quality-review passes:

| Workstream | Scope |
|---|---|
| **W1 — Stakeholders and governance** | Public-health authorities, meteorological agencies, local government, healthcare facilities, emergency services, community organisations, community health workers, caregivers, residents, employers, schools; roles, responsibilities, information needs, handoff relationships |
| **W2 — Stakeholder journey and contact points** | Detection → interpretation → alert creation → distribution → receipt → acknowledgement → protective action → escalation/fallback → feedback |
| **W3 — Communication channels and fallback** | Dashboards, push notifications, SMS, cell broadcast, messaging apps, phone, email, radio, TV, loudspeakers, community visits; digital exclusion, network failure, language, disability |
| **W4 — Service logistics and coordination risk** | Acknowledgement, escalation, response delay, unclear ownership, failed handoffs, contact-registry decay, alert fatigue, channel fragmentation, coordination friction |
| **W5 — Adoption, trust and behavioural response** | Comprehension, usefulness, trust, credibility, relevance, timeliness, risk perception, task completion, continued use, abandonment |
| **W6 — CSAT-style evaluation** | Established measures for usefulness, clarity, trust, timeliness, accessibility, actionability, satisfaction, channel preference |
| **W7 — Thailand and Southeast Asia** | LINE, SMS, cell broadcast, อสม./village health volunteers, local government, public announcements, older adults, migrant workers, outdoor workers, low-digital-literacy users |
| **QA1 — Consolidation** | De-duplication, canonical registry, downgrades, contradiction identification |
| **QA2 — Adversarial citation verification** | Independent re-verification of the 28 most load-bearing citations |

### Rules applied throughout

1. Every source was **opened** before being recorded. Nothing was cited from a search snippet.
2. Where full text could not be retrieved, the record states so and the claim is limited to what was actually seen.
3. DOIs were recorded only where the DOI string was literally visible on the retrieved page.
4. Statistics were quoted exactly as seen, with sample size and country where available.
5. Blogs, marketing pages, vendor pages, AI summaries, Wikipedia and opinion pieces were excluded as core evidence. **No source of these types entered the final registry.**
6. Where no Thailand-specific evidence existed, that was recorded as an explicit gap rather than substituted with a global finding.

## Remaining evidence gaps

### Gaps that no amount of further searching in this review would close

1. **No study measures acknowledgement rates in public-health mass notification.** Targeted searching returned only vendor material, which was excluded. All acknowledgement evidence in these deliverables is transferred from clinical alerting by explicit analogy.
2. **No heat-specific empirical test of warning fatigue exists.** The 2025 systematic review of heat health warning systems names it as an inadequately addressed gap.
3. **No operating heat warning system was documented performing receipt verification**, despite it being a formal early-warning requirement.
4. **No study quantifies contact-registry decay rates.**
5. **No source provides an evidence-based response-time or acknowledgement-window standard for a heat alert.** Every timing field in the contact-point matrix is therefore marked "To be defined and tested."
6. **No per-channel reach study exists for heat, anywhere.** The only quantified per-channel reach data located is for cyclone warning in Bangladesh.
7. **No per-channel cost data was located.**
8. **No direct evaluation of IVR or reverse-911 systems was located.**
9. **No instrument located has been validated for heat-warning services.**
10. **No validated instrument exists for timeliness, relevance or channel preference.** Trust is only partially covered, and the one trust-in-automation scale located was excluded (items not publicly available; independent psychometric rating 46%).

### Thailand-specific gaps — recorded explicitly so global findings are not passed off as local

1. **No evidence that อสม. (village health volunteers) have ever delivered a heat warning.** Searched in Thai and English; result null. **This is the project's largest unevidenced local assumption.**
2. **No study measures the reach, comprehension or behavioural effect of any Thai heat warning.**
3. **No Thai CSAT, adoption or usability study of any heat or disaster warning product.**
4. **No evidence on Thai village loudspeakers (หอกระจายข่าว).** The only loudspeaker-warning study located was from Guangdong, China, and contains nothing on Thailand.
5. **No evidence on temple announcements or community radio for Thai health communication.**
6. **Current operational status of Thai Cell Broadcast unverified.** The government portal said "expected early 2025" at time of publication.
7. **No credible LINE user statistic for Thailand.** All candidates were marketing material or Statista and were excluded. **Do not cite a LINE penetration number.**
8. **No peer-reviewed evaluation of หมอพร้อม / Mor Prom.** News reporting only.
9. **No NBTC-sourced penetration figures obtainable** (Tableau-rendered). NSO figures used instead.
10. **No nationwide count of รพ.สต. (sub-district health promoting hospitals) obtained.**
11. **No study evaluates migrant workers' access to Thai heat warnings.** The language exclusion in the national alert pipe is *inferable from the published language list*, not measured.
12. **No evidence on motorcycle taxi riders** as an occupational heat-risk group.
13. **No consolidated Thai heat action plan located.**
14. **The Thai heat-index band structure is unresolved** — five bands (top ≥54 °C, peer-reviewed) versus four bands (red >52 °C, Department of Health). Requires confirmation from a Thai authority.
15. **Two Thai official heat-death series do not reconcile** (139 deaths 2018–2023 from DDC; 131 deaths 2019–2023 from DoH).

### Two search results that were misleading and were caught only by opening them

Recorded as a method note: a Department of Disease Control URL that a snippet indicated was heat guidance turned out to be a recruitment notice, and a PubMed identifier returned in a heat-related search resolved to a paper on autophagy. Both would have entered the evidence base if snippet text had been trusted. **This is why every source was opened.**

## Inclusion and exclusion criteria applied

**Included** if: peer-reviewed; or published by WHO, WMO, UNDRR, ITU, a national government or public-health authority; or an official heat-health action plan or warning-system evaluation; or a systematic review; **and** the URL resolved and the content matched the claim.

**Downgraded to context only** if: derived from a press release, feature story or landing page rather than a report body; or an editorial; or based on a non-random, uncontrolled or single-site design being used to support a general claim; or from a country being cited as a comparator rather than as direct evidence.

**Excluded** if: a blog, marketing page, vendor page, AI summary, Wikipedia entry or opinion piece; or a practitioner magazine article whose central claim is refuted by peer-reviewed evidence and whose mechanics could not be verified; or an instrument whose items are not publicly available and whose psychometric evidence was independently rated as weak; or an institutional feature story whose figures are superseded by an official statistical release.

**Reputable news agencies** were permitted only to establish that a programme or event exists, clearly labelled as news reporting, and never for a statistic or a causal claim.
