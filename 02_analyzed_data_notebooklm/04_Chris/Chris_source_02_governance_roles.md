# Chris Source 02 — Empirical Taxonomy of Heat-Governance Roles

**Focus applied to this summary:** stakeholders, communication logistics, contact points, channels, handoffs, ownership, acknowledgement, fallback mechanisms, adoption, satisfaction and service usefulness. Contact-point level only.

## Citation

Vanderplanken, K., van den Hazel, P., Marx, M., Shams, A.Z., Guha-Sapir, D. & van Loenhout, J.A.F. (2021). Governing heatwaves in Europe: comparing health policy and practices to better understand roles, responsibilities and collaboration. *Health Research Policy and Systems*, 19, 20.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12961-020-00645-2
**Source ID:** CS-04 · **Source type:** Peer-reviewed mixed-methods article · **Full text:** YES (open access) · **Independently re-verified:** PASS — all seven role names and the "municipal level is rarely considered" wording confirmed verbatim.

## Research context

Desk review of **15 national heat–health action plans** plus **68 key informant interviews across nine countries** (Belgium, France, Germany, Lithuania, North Macedonia, Portugal, Spain, Switzerland, UK). This is the most directly on-point peer-reviewed empirical study of heat-governance roles located in this review — most other governance material is normative guidance rather than observation.

## Stakeholders

Meteorological agencies; health ministries and public health agencies; regional and municipal authorities; care providers (GPs, elderly care); emergency services; social services; educational institutions; NGOs including the Red Cross.

## Contact points

The study's central contribution is that contact points can be decomposed into **seven distinct governance roles**, each of which is a different kind of contact: **author** (writes the plan), **activator** (triggers it), **coordinator** (holds the response together), **monitor** (watches conditions and effects), **informer** (communicates outward), **implementer** (delivers the action), **evaluator** (assesses afterwards).

## Information transferred

Alert activation decisions; role assignments; threshold definitions. The study documents cross-agency threshold setting in practice — in France, alert "levels are defined by [the public health agency] and [the meteorological agency] jointly."

## Channels

Not the study's focus. It reports instead that **activators "are unsure how to adequately communicate activation to all external stakeholders and the public"** — the channel question is unresolved at the governance level, not only at the technical level.

## Handoffs and ownership

The most important finding for this project: **"Roles are mainly defined and assigned in relation to the alert levels of the warning system, causing other role aspects and other roles to be vague and ambiguous."** In other words, plans specify who does what *at each alert level* and leave everything outside that grid undefined. A second structural gap: **"NHHAPs are developed at the national level, but the implementation relies heavily on regional and local stakeholders," yet "the municipal level is rarely considered within NHHAPs."**

## Acknowledgement or escalation

Not measured. The study reports "a lack of good coordination, particularly at subnational levels."

## Failure points

- Role ambiguity outside the alert-level grid.
- The municipal implementation layer is largely absent from national plans.
- Inter-organizational collaboration "is usually limited to an organization's own network and do[es] not often go beyond the health sector."
- **Front-line implementers "lack the autonomy to execute certain roles when these deviate from their regular organizational tasks"** — being assigned a task is not the same as being permitted to perform it.

## Adoption or satisfaction findings

None directly, but the autonomy finding is an adoption constraint: a dashboard that assigns a task to a front-line worker who cannot act on it without permission will not produce the action.

## Relevance to this project

This paper supplies the vocabulary for `Chris_stakeholder_journey.md`. Rather than inventing role labels, HeatShield can map each journey stage to one of seven empirically derived roles. It also supplies the sharpest warning for the dashboard's "ownership, contact point, or handoff status" field: if HeatShield ties ownership only to the alert tier, it will reproduce exactly the ambiguity this study documents.

## Limitations

European only; self-reported interview data; nine countries interviewed against 15 plans reviewed, so the plan-versus-practice comparison is uneven; pre-2021 and does not reflect subsequent plan revisions. No Southeast Asian or Thai coverage.

## Supported claims

1. A comparative study of 15 European national heat–health action plans and 68 key informant interviews identified seven distinct governance roles — author, activator, coordinator, monitor, informer, implementer and evaluator.
2. Roles are mainly defined and assigned in relation to the alert levels of the warning system, causing other role aspects and other roles to be vague and ambiguous.
3. National heat plans rely on regional and local implementation, yet the municipal level is rarely considered within them.
4. Front-line implementers lack the autonomy to execute roles that deviate from their regular organizational tasks.
5. Inter-organizational collaboration is usually limited to an organization's own network and does not often extend beyond the health sector.
6. Cross-agency joint threshold setting between a public health agency and a meteorological agency exists in practice (France).
