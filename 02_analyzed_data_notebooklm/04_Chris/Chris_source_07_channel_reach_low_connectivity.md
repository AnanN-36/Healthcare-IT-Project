# Chris Source 07 — Per-Channel Warning Reach in a Low-Connectivity Setting

**Focus applied to this summary:** stakeholders, communication logistics, contact points, channels, handoffs, ownership, acknowledgement, fallback mechanisms, adoption, satisfaction and service usefulness. Contact-point level only.

## Citation

Hossain, M.L. (2026). Beyond warnings and shelters: local institutions and trust build cyclone resilience in Bangladesh. *npj Natural Hazards*, 3, 17.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s44304-026-00177-9
**Source ID:** CS-30 · **Source type:** Peer-reviewed multi-district household survey · **Full text:** YES (open access) · **Independently re-verified:** PARTIAL — percentages confirmed; article number corrected to 17; **the study is exclusively about tropical cyclone warnings and contains no heat content.**

> **Hazard-transfer caveat — apply to every citation of this source.** Cyclones are rapid-onset with short lead times; heat is slow-onset with days of warning. That difference changes the relative value of instantaneous channels. The channel-reach *ranking* below should be treated as **indicative for heat, not established.**

## Research context

Household survey across seven coastal districts of Bangladesh (Bhola, Satkhira, Patuakhali, Barguna, Pirojpur, Khulna, Bagerhat). It is the only source located in this review that **quantifies reach separately for each channel** in a low-connectivity setting — which is why it is retained despite the hazard mismatch.

## Stakeholders

Coastal households, including female-headed households, fishing versus farming communities, and island versus mainland communities; Cyclone Preparedness Programme (CPP) volunteers; Union Parishad (local government); religious institutions.

## Contact points

Volunteer-to-household (door-to-door and megaphone); broadcast-to-household; mosque announcement-to-congregation; local government-to-household; neighbour-to-neighbour.

## Information transferred

Cyclone warning and evacuation instruction.

## Channels — measured reach

| Channel | Reach |
|---|---|
| Trained CPP volunteers | **>72% in every district, peaking at 90% (Bhola)** |
| Mass media (TV/radio) | 30–67% |
| Religious institutions (mosque announcements) | 40–45% |
| Union Parishad (local government) | 27.5–55% |
| **Digital (SMS/social media)** | **17–40%** |
| Word of mouth | 10–25% |

**In this setting the digital channel was the worst performer and the trained human volunteer network was the best, by a factor of roughly 2–4×.**

## Handoffs and ownership

The volunteer network owns the last mile. The study's title makes the causal argument: local institutions and trust, not the warning technology, are what produced resilience.

## Acknowledgement or escalation

Not measured.

## Failure points

**Receipt is systematically unequal.** Male-headed households 76.6% versus female-headed **64.8%** (11.8-point gap); farming 82.5% versus fishing **70.0%** (12.5-point gap); island communities 3.3 points below mainland. Institutional trust varied from 3.5/5 (Barguna) down to 2.0/5 (Pirojpur).

## Adoption or satisfaction findings

**Receipt-to-action conversion was roughly half and was gated by whether the recommended action was feasible.** Evacuation ranged 33–68% across unions, mean **52.1%**, correlating strongly with *shelter quality*. The direct analogue for heat is cooling-centre access: telling someone to go somewhere cool only works if the somewhere is usable.

## Relevance to this project

This is the strongest quantitative case for treating a trained human network as a **primary** channel rather than a courtesy fallback in low-connectivity settings — directly relevant to the Thai village health volunteer (อสม.) question in CS-92 and CS-102. It also supplies the general principle that action conversion depends on the feasibility of the recommended action, not only on message quality.

## Limitations

**Cyclone, not heat.** Cross-sectional household survey; single country; self-reported receipt; published in 2026 with little time for critique to accumulate. Bangladesh's CPP is a mature, well-funded volunteer institution and the result may not transfer to weaker volunteer networks.

## Supported claims

1. In a low-connectivity setting, trained human volunteer networks reached >72% of households in every district (peaking at 90%), while digital channels (SMS/social media) reached only 17–40% — the worst-performing channel measured.
2. Religious/community announcement points are a measurable warning channel, reaching 40–45%.
3. Warning receipt was systematically lower for female-headed households (64.8% vs 76.6%) and for fishing versus farming communities (70.0% vs 82.5%).
4. Receipt-to-action conversion averaged 52.1% and correlated strongly with the quality of the shelter people were being asked to go to.
5. All of the above are cyclone-warning findings and are indicative, not established, for heat.
